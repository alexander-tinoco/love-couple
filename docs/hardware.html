01

Findings that change the order

You were right to do this first. Each of these would have cost days if it had surfaced with the parts already soldered.

1 · The accelerometer may not run on 3.3 V

The common GY-521 breakout of the MPU6050 carries an LDO regulator that wants 4.3–9 V in. Our only rail is 3.3 V. The XIAO does have a 5 V pin, but it is USB VBUS — on battery it is dead, so an IMU wired to it would work on the bench and stop working the moment you unplug. That is the worst kind of bug: it passes every test you would think to run.

What to do: it often works anyway — an LDO in dropout passes roughly 3.1–3.2 V through, and the MPU6050 die is rated 2.375–3.46 V. So test it in five minutes before designing around it: 3.3 V on VCC, read register 0x75 (WHO_AM_I) over I²C. If it answers 0x68 and stays stable while you shake it, ship it. If not, bridge the LDO's input to its output — it is a 3-pin SOT-23 — or buy a module without one. An LIS3DH or ADXL345 is 3.3 V native and, for shake, tilt and stillness, an accelerometer alone is all we ever use. We never needed the gyroscope.

2 · Charging takes nine hours, not four

The XIAO's charger is a BQ25101 set to 50 mA fast charge (3.8 mA trickle) — not the 100 mA I assumed in the build plan. A 400 mAh cell at 50 mA is 8 hours of constant current plus the top-off phase, so about 9. That is fine for something you drop on a nightstand, and it is gentle on the cell, but it is not "plug it in for an hour before you leave". The rate is set by a resistor on the board; changing it means reworking an 0402, which is not worth it.

3 · The display's pin header has to come off

The GC9A01 modules ship with a 2.54 mm header soldered on. That header is about 8 mm tall — taller than the display, the XIAO and the battery combined would need. Desolder it and run wires flat into the pads. This single step is roughly 40 % of the finished thickness, and it is far easier to do before anything else is attached.

02

The XIAO: what's taken, what's free

The Sense expansion board does not steal any of the eleven exposed pads — the camera and microphone are wired internally, on GPIOs that never reach the edge. What it does claim is the microSD slot, which sits on the SPI bus. Seeed's own wiki is blunt about it: use the card and you cannot use SPI. We are not using the card, so the bus is ours.

Where the sleep figure is soft

Seeed quote 14 µA deep sleep for the bare XIAO, and forum measurements broadly agree. But our 1.5 mA estimate is dominated by things that are not the ESP32: the display module keeps its regulator and backlight driver powered even with the panel asleep, and there is no way to cut it without adding a MOSFET on the 3V3 line.

Measure it in phase 2, with a meter in series with the battery. If it comes back above 3 mA, a single P-channel MOSFET switching the display's supply pays for itself several times over — and that is a change you want to make while the wires are still loose.

What actually eats the charge

  • The backlight, always. It is on whenever the display is, and PWM is the only lever. 60 % looks identical indoors and saves a third.
  • The camera, in bursts. Running it continuously roughly halves your runtime for an effect nobody can see faster than 10 fps.
  • WiFi, barely. A check-in every 30 minutes costs about 6 mAh a day — a tenth of the budget. Do not contort the design to avoid it.
  • Sleeping properly is worth more than all of it. The difference between light sleep at 5 mA and deep sleep at microamps is the difference between two days and nine.
06

How it all fits in a star

I ran the geometry before drawing anything, and it produced the one finding that changes a line item in the order. Two constraints collide:

The display module is 37.5 mm across and sits in the middle, so the star's waist — the concave points between the arms — has to clear it. That forces a very fat star: a normal five-point star has an inner radius around 45 % of its outer one, and we need 67 % at Ø 62 mm. That is fine, and it is exactly why the real STARBOY is such a chunky, rounded star rather than a spiky one.

But the second constraint has no such escape. Laying the board and the battery side by side takes a 41 × 35 mm footprint, which needs a Ø 54 mm clear circle. A Ø 60 mm star only offers Ø 41.5 mm between its waist and its walls. They do not fit in one layer. The battery has to go behind the board — and that is where the thickness comes from.

Two millimetres for 50 mAh is a good trade on something that hangs off a pocket. Going to 3 mm costs a full day of runtime for one more millimetre — not worth it.
Why the real one is 12 mm and ours will not be

STARBOY is about 12 mm thick because CREATURE had a custom PCB with the panel bonded straight to it and a cell shaped to the cavity. With off-the-shelf modules you are carrying a second PCB under the display, a 2.54 mm-pitch board under that, and a rectangular pouch cell — roughly 6 mm of packaging that a custom design simply does not have. Around 18 mm is the honest floor for this approach. It is still a keychain, and it is thinner than most car fobs.

Assembly order — it only works in this sequence
  1. Desolder the display header first. Impossible once anything else is attached, and it is 8 mm of your budget.
  2. Solder all eleven wires to the display and the accelerometer while the boards are loose on the bench, and test the whole thing working flat before it goes anywhere near a printed part.
  3. Battery last, and only after every other joint is verified. Once the cell is on you cannot use a hot iron near it safely.
  4. Glue the crystal into the bezel, and only then close the case. Adhesive over a mounted display is a mistake you cannot undo.
Measure, don't trust this drawing

Every figure above comes from datasheets and vendor listings, not from calipers on your parts. Module thicknesses vary by supplier by a millimetre or more, and 1 mm out of 18 is 6 % of the object. Do not finalise the CAD until the parts are in your hands — model the cavity from measurements, and print the bezel alone three times before you commit to a full enclosure.

07

Getting on WiFi once it's sealed

You asked the right question. Once the case is closed there is no keyboard, no screen you can type on, and no USB port you want to open up at her house. Hard-coding the networks works until she takes it somewhere new, and then the link is simply dead.

Do this

Captive portal

Hold BOOT through the pinhole at power-on and the keychain becomes a WiFi access point called starboy-setup. Connect a phone to it, a page opens by itself, pick the network, type the password, done. The WiFiManager library is about fifteen lines and stores the credentials in flash. No app, no cable, works from anyone's phone, and it is the same flow every smart plug on the market uses — she will already know how.

Also good

Ask its twin

Both keychains already speak ESP-NOW. Once yours knows a network, it can hand the credentials to hers the next time they are close. Lovely, and genuinely useful when they meet somewhere new — but it needs the portal to exist first for the very first setup, so it is an addition, not a replacement.

Skip

BLE provisioning

The S3 has Bluetooth and Espressif ship a provisioning stack for it, but it wants a companion app or WebBluetooth, which is Chrome-on-Android only. More moving parts than the portal for an identical result.

Two details that will bite otherwise

Reaching the Worker over HTTPS. The ESP32 has to validate Cloudflare's certificate. Either pin the root CA in flash, or call setInsecure() on the WiFiClientSecure. Pinning is correct but the certificate expires and then your keychain silently stops syncing; setInsecure() is fine here because the payload is a mood and a timestamp, and a Worker endpoint with a random path is not worth attacking. Take the pragmatic one and write down why.

Never block on the network. If the WiFi is gone the connect attempt can hang for seconds, and a keychain whose eyes freeze while it looks for a router is broken as far as anyone watching is concerned. Give it a hard timeout, run it between frames, and let the animation keep going regardless.

08

The app

You floated a mobile app for the counters and the connection status. It is a good idea and I would build it — but not as an app.

The Worker in the build plan already holds everything an app would show: last seen, days apart, mood, battery, whether either one is online. Serving a page from that same Worker gets you all of it for an afternoon of work and zero new infrastructure. Add it to the phone's home screen and it has an icon and opens full-screen with no browser chrome. She will not be able to tell it from an app, and you will not have spent a week on build tooling and store accounts three weeks before her birthday.

Safety

Short list. Most of it is the battery, because most of it is always the battery.

RuleWhy
Never reverse the LiPoDestroys the XIAO instantly and can vent the cell. Negative sits toward the USB connector. Check with a meter before the first connection, not after.
Never compress the cellA pouch cell under pressure from a screw boss or a tight wall is the one genuine fire risk in this build. 1 mm of air on every face, and nothing rigid pressing on it.
Never solder near a connected cellBattery goes on last, comes off first if you need to rework anything.
Nothing above 3.3 V downstreamThe display has no regulator and no level shifting. 5 V on its VCC is instant and permanent.
Don't charge it below 0 °CLithium plating. The BQ25101 here has no temperature sense, so it will happily do the wrong thing.
Don't leave the first charge unattendedWatch the first full cycle. If the cell warms noticeably above room temperature, stop and check your wiring.
Keep the crystal off the panel0.3 mm of air. Pressure on an LCD leaves permanent marks, and you will not notice until it is glued shut.
Strain-relieve every wireEleven AWG30 wires in a pocket-sized object flex every time it is handled. A wire that breaks inside a sealed case is a full teardown.
10

What to do before ordering

Nothing here blocks the order except one line item — and that one is worth the ten minutes.

Order unchanged: 3× XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense, 3× GC9A01 display, the crystals and the hardware. All of it verified against the datasheets above.

Change one — the battery. Swap the 602035 for a 402535, about 350 mAh. Two millimetres thinner for 50 mAh less, on the only layer of the stack that is still negotiable. It takes the object from 20 mm to 18 mm, which is the difference between "chunky" and "fine". Same price bracket, same JST connector.

Change two — the accelerometer. Either add a note to test the MPU6050 at 3.3 V the day it arrives and rework the LDO if it fails, or spend the same money on a 3.3 V-native LIS3DH or ADXL345 and skip the risk entirely. UNIT did not list either when I searched, so MercadoLibre is the place to look. We only ever use the accelerometer — the gyroscope in the MPU6050 has no job in this design.

And add one thing that is not on the list: a cheap USB power meter or a multimeter that can read microamps. Every battery figure in section 05 is an estimate, the sleep current is the one that decides whether this lasts nine days or two, and it is the only number you cannot look up — it depends on your exact display module. Measuring it in phase 2 is what turns this document from a plan into a fact.
