Why it looked too perfect

  
I found CREATURE's generator at creature.company/eyes, which draws every one of the 400 looks live. Four things were wrong, and all four were making it read as machine-drawn.


  

    

      
1. The two eyes are not the same size

      
This is the big one. In every single look, one eye is noticeably larger than the other and sits at a slightly different height. I had been drawing a perfect mirror image, which is exactly what makes something look machine-drawn. A 10–20 % difference is enough — it reads as a face, not a diagram.

    

    

      
2. Superellipses, not rounded rectangles

      
A rounded rectangle has straight sides between its corners, and at this size your eye finds them. The real bodies are superellipses — one exponent controls the whole silhouette, from a plump circle to a soft square, with no straight segment anywhere. It cannot accidentally look rectangular.

    

    

      
3. The inner edge is flattened

      
The two bodies nestle against each other with a flat facing edge, like two stones pressed together. It stops them reading as two separate circles floating in the dark, and it is what gives the pair a single silhouette.

    

    

      
4. The pupil is small and off-centre

      
In the generator most pupils are tiny dots — under a fifth of the body width — and they sit off toward the inner top, at slightly different spots in each eye. A big centred pupil looks like a target. A small offset one looks like it is thinking about something.

    

  


  

    Also from the site, worth knowing
    
CREATURE's own copy says starboys “swap eyes, spread behaviours, and play with each other” when they meet. So the thing that travels between two devices is not the creature — it is the look. That is far simpler to build than the hand-off you already cut, and it is a much better fit for two keychains: after they have been together a while, each one is wearing a little of the other's face.
