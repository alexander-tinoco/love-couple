Eye editor

The eyes are drawn from ten numbers, not from exported animations. Tune them here, see them on a true-size display, and copy the C++ straight into the firmware. It is tuned to match the original STARBOY: big, nearly elliptical shapes tilted inwards, with a gradient, a glow and spring physics.

What makes it look like the original

  
I compared it frame by frame against the video. It comes down to six things, and none of them is “more animations”.


  

    

      
1. The shape, not the drawing

      
In the original the eyes are big and nearly elliptical, taking up more than half the display width, and tilted inwards by about 12°. My first version was small, upright rounded rectangles — which is why it read as a cheap toy. Push “Width” and “Roundness” until they are almost ovals: the change is instant.

    

    

      
2. Gradient, not flat colour

      
A flat fill reads as an LED. A vertical gradient — lighter at the top, darker at the bottom — reads as volume. On the firmware you do not need a real gradient: paint the body in the base colour and lay a lighter shape over the top 45 %. At this size nobody can tell.

    

    

      
3. Glow against black

      
The halo around the shape is what makes it look like it emits light rather than being painted on. On the ESP32 that is three concentric rounded rectangles drawn before the main shape, each 4 px larger and blended further toward black. It costs microseconds.

    

    

      
4. Anti-aliased edges

      
This is the most important item on the list and the easiest to skip. Without anti-aliasing, a curve at 240×240 looks like a staircase and the whole effect collapses. TFT_eSPI has had fillSmoothRoundRect() since version 2.5 — always use it, never fillRoundRect().

    

    

      
5. Spring, not linear interpolation

      
A plain lerp reaches the target and stops: it reads as mechanical. A spring overshoots slightly and settles. It is three lines, and it is the difference between looking like a UI and looking like a creature. Toggle the “Spring” button while switching moods and you will see it.

    

    

      
6. Squash and stretch

      
Moving fast, the eye lengthens along the direction of travel and thins perpendicular to it; when it stops, it bounces. It is the oldest principle in Disney animation — and the people who made STARBOY came from exactly there. Area is preserved, so it never looks like it is growing.

    

  


  

    And the detail nobody looks at but everybody notices
    
The blink is asymmetric: 60 ms to close, 120 ms to open, because that is how real eyes work. And the second eye trails the first by a couple of frames when the gaze moves. Neither is consciously noticed, but remove them and everyone says it “looks off” without being able to say why. Both are active here.
