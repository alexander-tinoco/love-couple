Check before soldering

The XIAO Sense's microSD slot shares the SPI bus (D8/D9/D10): as long as you never call SD.begin() there's no conflict, but don't mount the card. And check this table against the official pinout for your board revision before you touch it with solder.

04

Yes, you need glass

The GC9A01 module comes with its own panel glass, but that isn't enough: it's thin and untreated, and this is a keychain — it will live rubbing against metal keys. It'll be scratched within a week. On top of that the module sits recessed inside the printed hole, and that step is exactly what makes an object look homemade.

What you want is a flat mineral watch crystal: Ø 34–36 mm, 1 mm thick. It's the exact part watches use, it shrugs off keys, and it costs pocket change at any watch-repair shop.

Pick this one

Mineral

Around 5 on the Mohs scale. Ø 34–36 mm × 1 mm, flat. Buy four: one will crack and another will end up glued crooked. Guaranteed.

Overkill

Sapphire

Better in every way, but I found one on Amazon MX at $339 a piece. You don't need it for this.

Avoid

Acrylic

Scratches about as easily as no cover at all. Fine for a desk prototype, wrong for something that lives with keys.

How to mount it

Cut a 1.1 mm recess in the bezel, sized to the crystal diameter plus 0.2 mm, so it sits flush with the outer surface. That flushness is the whole difference.

Glue only around the perimeter, with black double-sided tape 0.05–0.1 mm thick cut into a ring. Never put adhesive over the active area: any bubble is there forever. And leave 0.3 mm of air between crystal and panel — if they touch, the pressure leaves marks on the LCD.

05

The camera

Dropping face recognition makes the camera much simpler — and you lose almost nothing of what people actually see.

esp-dl ships a pre-trained face detector inside the ESP32-S3, running at 5–10 fps. Nothing to train, no fallback plan, no decision date. It returns a bounding box, and that box gives you four behaviours:

Someone's there

Wakes up and pays attention.

Where they are

The eyes follow you. This alone is the effect you were after.

How close

If the box grows suddenly, it flinches back in surprise.

How many people

Three or more faces in a row → it gets overwhelmed.

What you gain by cutting it

Face recognition was the only part of the plan with a mandatory fallback (Edge Impulse) and its own decision date (16 September). Cutting it removes the last real technical risk and frees six days. They go into the animations.

06

The link

Two transports for the same idea, and the keychain picks one on its own depending on where the other is. Close, they talk directly over ESP-NOW — no router, no cost. Far, each one over its own WiFi, through a Cloudflare Worker that just stores the last state of each.

The deliberate trigger is no longer shaking — you were right, a keychain rattles all day — but looking at it head-on. The camera sees a face, and that never happens inside a pocket. Her keychain sees a face → yours finds out. It reuses the exact same detector, without a single new line of code.

How long since you last met

This comes free from ESP-NOW, with nothing to configure: the last time the two keychains saw each other up close is the last time the two of you were together.

  • Under 6 h → normal
  • 6 h to 1 day → glances at the door now and then
  • 1 to 3 days → misses you
  • Over 3 days → sad, and shows the day count
  • Reunion → party animation, counter back to zero

Days-together counter, and noise

A number that climbs on its own from whatever date you pick — your anniversary, or 26 September itself so it starts at zero the day she gets it. The XIAO has no real-time clock: sync over NTP whenever it connects and store it; between connections millis() is far more than accurate enough to count days.

The PDM microphone is already on board. Read only the RMS level ten times a second — no FFT, no speech. Sudden noise → it startles. Loud sustained noise → it gets overwhelmed. Silence plus stillness → it falls asleep. Thirty lines.

They swap eyes when they meet

CREATURE's own copy says starboys “swap eyes, spread behaviours, and play with each other” when they come into contact in the wild. That is worth stealing, and it is the good version of the hand-off you cut earlier.

What travels is not the creature — it is the look: eye shape, asymmetry, pupil size and tone. In the editor that is a struct of ten bytes. Two keychains inside ESP-NOW range trade looks, and each keeps a blend of what it received.

  • No two-phase commit, no rescue timers, nothing that can be lost. A look is copied, never moved, so a dropped packet costs nothing and the worst case is “nothing happened”.
  • Each device is born with its own look, seeded from its MAC address. Hers and yours start visibly different.
  • On every meeting, blend about 15 % of the other's look into your own. After a weekend together they start to resemble each other; apart, they slowly drift back.
  • That drift is the whole gift. The keychains end up carrying a visible record of how much time you have actually spent together — for about thirty lines on top of the ESP-NOW layer you are already building.
07

The animations

You asked whether some skill or MCP could generate these. There isn't one — Canva and friends make graphics, not firmware sprites. So I built the tool instead, and with the six days freed by cutting face recognition, I tuned it to match the original.

Eye Editor

Open it here → A round display at true size, 16 moods, shape, pupil and style sliders, spring physics, and C++ export with the complete drawEyes() function. It needs no hardware: you can design all 16 moods today, while the boards are still in the post.

I compared it frame by frame against the STARBOY video. The gap with my first version wasn't "more animations" — it was a short list of specific things, and all of them are in the editor now:

WhatWhy it shows, and how to do it on the ESP32
1. The asymmetryCREATURE publish their eye-look generator at creature.company/eyes, and it settles the question: in every one of the 400 looks, one eye is visibly larger than the other and sits slightly lower. Perfectly mirrored eyes are what make something look machine-drawn. A 10–20 % difference is enough to turn a diagram into a face.
1b. SuperellipsesThe bodies are superellipses with a flattened inner edge, so the pair nestles into one silhouette. A rounded rectangle has straight sides between its corners and at this size the eye finds them; a superellipse has no straight segment anywhere. One exponent controls the whole outline.
2. The pupilEvery reference photo shows the same structure: a large coloured body with a second, clearly different shape inside it. The pupil is what gives a direction of gaze — and what makes size mean something: a tiny pupil in a wide eye reads instantly as shock. The fills themselves are flat; the soft shading in the photos is the dome glass, not the pixels.
3. GlowThe halo is what makes it look like it emits light rather than being painted on. Three concentric rounded rectangles before the main shape, each 4 px larger and blended further toward black with tft.alphaBlend(). Costs microseconds.
4. Anti-aliasingThe most important item here and the easiest to skip. Without it, a curve at 240×240 looks like a staircase and the whole effect collapses. Use fillSmoothRoundRect() from TFT_eSPI 2.5+, never fillRoundRect().
5. Spring, not lerpA lerp reaches the target and stops: it reads as mechanical. A spring overshoots slightly and settles. Three lines: vel += (target−cur)*0.20; vel *= 0.70; cur += vel;
6. Squash and stretchMoving fast, the eye lengthens along the direction of travel and thins perpendicular to it, preserving area. It's the oldest principle in Disney animation — and the STARBOY people came from exactly there.
7. The pair wandersThe creature looks around by moving its whole face across the display, not just its pupils. Two systems at two speeds: a slow, heavy drift that carries both bodies together, and a faster, lighter one that nudges the body a little and the pupil a lot. Run them at the same rate and it reads as a slider being dragged.
8. One clock per transitionDo not give each parameter its own spring. Fourteen springs ringing at once means the eyelid arrives before the body and the brow overshoots alone, and the face passes through poses that belong to no mood — it reads as broken. One eased clock over ~430 ms, with only the four size parameters allowed to overshoot.
9. Asleep means asleepNo wandering, no gaze, no cursor, no breathing. And snap the residual motion to exactly zero instead of letting the springs approach it: springs never quite arrive, which leaves a permanent shimmer and means the device can never stop redrawing. A genuinely static frame is also a battery decision.
And the two details nobody looks at but everybody notices

The blink is asymmetric — 60 ms to close, 120 ms to open, the way real eyes work — and it fires at irregular intervals, because a fixed interval is detectable within seconds. And the second eye trails the first by a couple of frames when the gaze moves. Remove either one and everyone says it "looks off" without being able to say why.

On top of the eyes, hand-draw only four or five small sprites: hearts, zzz, a question mark, the "missing you" mark and the cake for the 26th. That's the entire drawing workload of the project — one afternoon in Aseprite.

And the number that matters: all 16 moods together take about 500 bytes. As sprites they'd be several megabytes, and you couldn't interpolate between them.

Black enclosure

Black solves the worry you started with all on its own: you're no longer competing against a chrome finish. Matte black with a round watch crystal sitting flush in the middle is exactly the language of expensive consumer electronics.

TopicRule
Textured platePrint the outer faces against a textured PEI plate. Uniform matte that hides the layer lines — in black it's indistinguishable from an injection-moulded part. Highest return on this list, and free.
Fuzzy skin0.15 mm on the side walls only. Never on the front face.
Three piecesFront bezel (crystal recess + lens hole) · mid chassis (board posts + battery cavity) · back cover (split-ring slot + USB-C cutout + BOOT pinhole).
Tolerances0.20 mm clearance for a sliding fit, 0.15 mm for a snug one. Crystal recess: diameter + 0.2 mm, 1.1 mm deep.
Walls1.6 mm minimum (4 perimeters at 0.4). Below 1.2 mm it breaks in a pocket.
The split ringAlways the part that snaps. Print it lying flat in XY so the layers aren't loaded in tension, cross-section ≥3 mm. Better still: a slot with a real metal ring in it.
BatteryAn 8 × 22 × 37 mm cavity for the 602035, with 1 mm of air. Never compress a LiPo with a screw or a tight wall. The only genuine safety rule here.
USB-CMeasure the actual connector (~9.0 × 3.2 mm) and add 0.4 mm per side.
Camera lensHole 0.3 mm larger than the barrel, with an internal shoulder so the module seats flat.
Quality0.12 mm layers, 0.2 mm nozzle if you have one.
